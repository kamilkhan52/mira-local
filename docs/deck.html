Preparation notes

Slide 1 · Scoring every paper with the LLM costs $30 of a weekly digest's $36

1.1 The pipeline

This is the n8n workflow's logic, unchanged in the local rebuild. Every paper gets two LLM calls, credibility from the affiliations on its first page and relevance with a topic, score and key findings. Papers that pass the profile's thresholds are ranked by score, and an LLM picks 20 of the top 100 for deep analysis and the report.

Is 2,000 every paper of the week?
No. 2,000 is the memory profile's arXiv cap, and this week reached it. A typical memory week has about 1,300 papers, where the gate saves about 53% instead of 57% because selection costs the same.
Which LLM does the scoring?
openai/gpt-5.6-terra through OpenRouter, as set in the profile.

1.2 $35.68 and 25 minutes on the local rebuild

The run made every LLM call, with no cached answers. Per-paper calls cost $30.21. Selection, deep analysis, the report, trend analysis and the news summary cost $5.46.

Why the local rebuild and not n8n?
n8n's four attempts at this week's digest on 30 Sep crashed after paper selection, an intermittent fault that other runs that day did not hit. Up to selection, n8n's per-paper scoring cost $29.77, close to the local run's.
Does the local rebuild produce the same digest as n8n?
Replaying n8n's daily digest of 26 Sep with n8n's recorded LLM answers gave byte-identical prompts and email.

1.3 The current LLM passed about 1 in 9 papers

In this week's rescore the current LLM, gpt-5.6-terra, passed 217 of 1,923 papers (11%). On 300 random past memory papers the earlier LLM, Gemini 3.1 Pro, passed 16%. The arXiv query selects by category, not by keyword, so most papers are outside the profile.

Are the rejected papers off-topic, or just not credible?
Mostly off-topic. Of the 300 past papers, the earlier LLM failed 83% on relevance and only 5% on credibility.

1.4 Decision model

Jev answers relevance with a probability for each of five levels. The cutoff applies to the expected level, a number from 0 to 4 computed from those probabilities.

Isn't a decision model a smaller LLM?
Yes, in size. Kev-9B is a 9B LLM, but it returns a probability for each answer instead of generating text, and a cutoff on the expected level computed from those probabilities is what a filter needs.

1.5 Jev's 16 seconds and 17 cents, and Kev-9B

Jev charges $0.042 per million input tokens. In the gate run it asked several questions per paper, about 2,000 tokens, which comes to 17 cents. Each paper is one request carrying all its questions. A request takes about 0.1 s, and 16 run at a time, so 2,000 papers take about 16 seconds. Kev-9B answers the same questions on our own hardware (slide 4).

Slide 2 · The gate dropped 5 of the 217 papers the current LLM passed this week

2.1 Prescreen

Set on one calibration sample and tested on the other, it kept 91 of 91 and 91 of 92 other LLM-passed papers and every past-digest paper. This week it screened out 45% of papers.

Why keep it if gate saves more?
For a profile where a missed paper is costly. It saves 37% of the cost instead of 57%.

2.2 Gate, the recommended level

Papers above the cutoff still get both LLM calls, so the gate can screen out a paper but never promote one. In 300 random calibration papers it screened out 73%, and this week 69% of the 2,000.

How was the week rescored?
The current LLM scored the week's papers: 1,923 succeeded, 232 of them reused from the 26 Sep daily digest, and 77 failed and are left out. Jev scored the 217 it passed at the gate cutoff. Of the 5 dropped, two scored 6, which can reach a shortlist with high credibility, and three scored 5.
Where do 90–92% and 98–100% come from?
The cutoff set on one sample kept 82 of 91 and 85 of 92 other LLM-passed papers in the other, and 79 of 81 and 83 of 83 past-digest papers. On both samples together it keeps 92% and 99%.
Why 98% this week but 90–92% in calibration?
Calibration used the earlier LLM's judgments. Against the current LLM's judgments the gate kept 212 of 217. The cutoffs should be recalibrated on the current LLM (slide 5).
Can it keep more?
A lower cutoff keeps more papers and saves less. It is one setting per profile.

2.3 Replace

The decision model scores relevance for every paper and passes or fails it at its own calibrated cutoff, then scores credibility from the first page for those that pass (440 this week). Its expected level is mapped onto the LLM's 1–10 bands, and these scores, not the LLM's, set the ranking.

What does the digest lose?
The picks lose their LLM-extracted affiliation lists and one-line key findings. Deep analysis still reads each pick in full.
Why did 440 papers (22%) pass replace when the current LLM passed 1 in 9?
Replace's cutoff was set to match the earlier LLM, which passed 16–17% of past random papers. This week had more papers above every cutoff than the calibration samples (gate 31% against 27%), so it reads as a more on-topic week.

2.4 Expect a few borderline papers a week to drop

This week it was 5 of 217. Calibration against the earlier LLM suggests up to 8–10% of LLM-passed papers, mostly scored 5–7 against a threshold of 5. Selection, deep analysis and the report are the same LLM steps at every level.

2.5 Calibration on 520–600 past LLM judgments

Production runs cache every LLM judgment. For each profile the benchmark draws two disjoint samples of random papers plus extra passing and past-pick ones. It scores them with the decision model and sets the cutoff to keep at least 95% of sampled LLM-passed papers (gate), or 99% and every past pick (prescreen), on both samples.

Were the cutoffs tuned on the test week?
No. They come only from the calibration samples.
Which profiles are calibrated?
Memory, cxl, storage and optical. Only memory has run end to end. mcbu-memory and embedded intelligence, added in July, are not calibrated yet.
Do the cutoffs go stale?
Yes, and one change has already happened: the samples hold Gemini 3.1 Pro's judgments, and the digest moved to gpt-5.6-terra on 1 Sep. Recalibrating on terra is the first step on slide 5. A profile focus edit also calls for one run.

Slide 3 · The gate cuts cost 57%, with overlap like a rerun's

3.1 Cost bars

Per-paper scoring falls with the share of papers each level screens out. Selection, analysis and the report cost about $5.50 at every level, and $5.05 at replace, whose selection prompt is shorter.

Are these the billed amounts?
No. The prescreen and gate runs started within an hour of the all-LLM run with identical prompts, and OpenRouter's prompt cache billed their per-paper calls 36–37% less. Gate was billed $11.84. A normal week gets no such discount, so every level is priced at the all-LLM run's cost per call.
Why is the replace bar all orange?
Replace makes no per-paper LLM calls. Its 19 cents of decision-model calls are too small to show.

3.2 The overlap table

Each range covers three all-LLM scorings of the week: the local run, n8n's and the rescore. They share 84–86 shortlisted papers with each other. Prescreen and gate share 82–87 with them, replace 53–55. Picks come from the local and n8n runs only, since the rescore did not run selection.

Did the gate drop a paper that should have been picked?
No. The three all-LLM picks the gate did not pick were all on its shortlist, so the final LLM selection left them out, not the gate.
Is three runs enough for a reference?
It is a small sample from one week, but the three agree closely (84–86). The LLM runs at the provider's default temperature, as in n8n, so its scores vary between runs.
Replace shares 17–18 of the 20 picks. Why not use it?
It shares only 53–55 shortlisted papers, because its scores set the order. Its picks may drift in other weeks, and they lose their affiliations and key findings. It would need several side-by-side weeks first.

3.3 Run times

Run time falls with the papers passed on. Selection and deep analysis of 20 papers take about 6–7 minutes at every level.

3.4 One memory week

n8n and the 30 Sep local runs scored the same 2,000 papers. No ground truth says which 20 papers are best, so the reference is what the all-LLM workflow itself picks.

Do readers agree the picks are as good?
Not tested. A reader review of the differing picks would be the next check.
Do the papers the gate loses cluster in one topic?
Not checked.

Slide 4 · Kev-9B could run in-house, with overlap close to Jev's

4.1 Fully local, and today's prices

Kev removes TypeSafe, which receives titles, abstracts and the profile's focus. The $1.88 is at today's OpenRouter prices and excludes the GPU. The LLM calls must also stay inside, through an in-house LLM gateway (slide 5).

Who set the fully local requirement?
It is the project's goal. IT did not approve n8n, and its replacement is meant to be completely local and licensed for commercial use, with no company data leaving Micron.
Has sending the focus text to TypeSafe been reviewed?
No review is on record, and TypeSafe's retention terms have not been checked. The trial live news agent on the development Mac already calls Jev hourly. It should be paused until the review.
Is the data sensitive?
The papers are public. The profile's focus, what Micron is looking for, is the part that reveals interest.

4.2 The models

Kev-9B v2 (jaredpalmer/kev, released 30 Sep) and Bespoke-Nimble-9B are small adapters on the open Qwen3.5-9B models, trained to answer the same kind of question as Jev through the same interface. Adapters and base models are Apache-2.0. Each got its own cutoffs from the same past LLM judgments and ran the gate level on the same week.

Why Kev over Nimble?
Kev screens out more papers (62% against 51%), so it costs $3.13 less a week. Kev's server batches requests on a GPU, per its authors. In our Ollama 0.35 setup Nimble served one request at a time. It ranks closer to the LLM than Nimble on cxl, storage and optical, and less close on memory (0.67 against 0.71). Nimble keeps more LLM-passed papers (93–96% against 87–97%).
Who maintains Kev, and what was it trained on?
An open-source project, mainly by one author, with versioned weights, so a deployment pins one release. Its model card lists public decision datasets (BoolQ, AG News, MNLI and others), developer-tool and document data, none of them a paper corpus.
Is a release from 30 Sep mature enough?
v2 is a further-trained release of an earlier Kev-9B. The side-by-side week on the Micron GPU is the check before switching.

4.3 Cost and papers screened out

Kev passed 768 papers to the LLM and Jev 627. The extra per-paper calls cost $2.15. Kev pays no Jev fee (17 cents), and its selection and analysis cost $0.10 less, so the net is $1.88. Nimble passed 983, a net $5.01 more than Jev.

Do these costs hold in the fully local setup?
No. They use OpenRouter prices for gpt-5.6-terra. An in-house LLM changes the per-call cost and needs recalibration (prep 5.4).
What does the GPU add?
Not measured. At the authors' L40S rate of about $1.95 an hour, a few minutes of scoring a week costs under a dollar on a rented GPU.

4.4 Papers kept, picks and rank agreement

Kev's held-out recall varies more than Jev's (87% and 97% of LLM-passed papers against 90% and 92%). Its shortlist overlap (82–87) and picks are close to the rerun range (84–86 and 17). Rank agreement, measured against the earlier LLM on random papers, is within 0.09 of Jev's: 0.09 below on cxl and level on optical.

4.5 Speed

On the Mac (M4 Pro, shared with other heavy work, two requests at a time), Kev took 60 minutes and Nimble 86. Kev's authors report 33 requests a second for Kev-9B on one L40S and 80 on an H100, for short texts. Our requests average about 550 tokens, so expect a few minutes for the week.

What GPU does Kev need?
About 17 GB of GPU memory, an L40S or H100 class card.
What if the Kev server is down?
If it refuses connections, each paper falls through to the LLM after a few retries, so the week runs at all-LLM cost about 25 minutes later. A hung server would stall the run, so the deployment needs a health check before each run.

Slide 5 · Approve the gate, and pilot Kev-9B for the local deployment

5.1 Gate level with Jev in the local rebuild

TypeSafe would receive titles, abstracts and the profile's focus, so it needs a data-handling review first. Until then the digest stays all-LLM. The level is one setting per run, so switching back is immediate.

What is the ask?
Approve the gate level and start the TypeSafe data-handling review. Owners and dates are to be agreed in the meeting.
Why use Jev at all instead of going straight to Kev?
Jev is measured end to end and works today. Kev needs a GPU and the pilot first. If the review is slow, the digest stays all-LLM until Kev is ready.
When does the rebuild go live?
Not decided yet.
What comes after?
Calibrating Kev for the trial hourly news alerts, which now score with Jev against a threshold set for Jev, and calibrating mcbu-memory and embedded intelligence, one run each.
Does the gate go into n8n?
No. It is part of the local rebuild, the planned replacement for n8n for these digests.
How would we notice a dropped paper later?
Run one all-LLM week per quarter beside the gate and compare, as in this test.
Could we save without a decision model?
Running relevance first and credibility only on papers that pass would cut per-paper cost by about half. It changes the n8n workflow's order, which this work kept unchanged, and is worth testing.
What does switching take?
The code is done. The work is the review, one side-by-side run per profile, and for the Micron deployment the GPU and gateway setup.

5.2 Recalibrate, then side-by-side runs for the other profiles

The cutoffs were set on the earlier LLM's judgments, so the first step is one calibration run per profile on gpt-5.6-terra. Then one gate run beside an all-LLM run over the same papers, a week for storage and optical and a month for cxl. A profile switches when the overlap is in the rerun range.

5.3 Pilot Kev-9B on a Micron GPU

Kev's server starts with one command from its repository, and the workflow points at it with a setting. Its speed and GPU cost are still to be measured.

What if Kev is slow on the GPU?
The weekly digest is not time-critical, and even the Mac's hour fits an overnight run.
What would the pilot need to show?
Scoring a week within the overnight window, and a side-by-side week whose overlap with all-LLM is in the rerun range.

5.4 In-house LLM gateway and recalibration

The workflow's LLM endpoint is a setting. A gateway keeps the calls inside only if it serves the models in-house. The cutoffs were set on the earlier LLM's judgments and are to be recalibrated on gpt-5.6-terra. An in-house LLM would need the same, its own judgments on about 600 papers per profile.

Does such a gateway exist?
Not known yet. It needs checking with IT.
Has OpenRouter been reviewed?
No review is on record either. The current setup sends the same text to OpenRouter, which is why the fully local deployment needs the gateway.

5.5 The three numbers

If all four calibrated profiles run on schedule, past production digests suggest about 230,000 papers a year (typically memory 1,300 a week, storage 1,500 a week, optical 500 a week, cxl 5,000 a month). At this week's per-paper rate that is about $3,500 of per-paper LLM scoring, and each profile's calibrated gate saving gives about $2,650, of which memory is about $745. At recent volumes (memory at its cap, cxl at 6,245 a month) the saving is about $3,300. Kev saves about the same as Jev.

What is 5 of 217?
This week's rescore on slide 2: the gate dropped 5 of the papers the current LLM passed, all scored 5–6.
Do all four profiles run on schedule today?
No. On n8n only the cxl monthly digest is scheduled. The weekly memory, storage and optical digests have run as backfills and manual runs.
Is the per-paper rate the same for every profile?
Assumed. Only memory has a measured all-LLM run.
Is that worth the work?
The money is modest. The larger gains are a 41% shorter run, room to raise the paper caps, and a scoring step that can stay inside Micron.
Why is the memory figure lower than this week's saving times 52?
This week hit the 2,000-paper cap. A typical memory week has about 1,300 papers.
